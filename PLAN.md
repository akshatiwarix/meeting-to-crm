---

## Problem

Every sales call produces information a CRM needs — who was on it, what stage the
deal is really at, what was promised, what happens next — but getting that
information from the call into structured fields is a manual, inconsistent, and
often-skipped step. Three failures show up over and over:

**1. Extraction is manual and inconsistent.** Reps write notes under time
pressure, right after (or during) a call. Names get misspelled, next steps go
unlogged, stage updates lag the actual conversation by days. The CRM record
reflects what a rep remembered to type, not what was said.

**2. When extraction is automated, its quality is invisible.** A tool that says
"here's your structured record" without showing *where* each fact came from is
asking for blind trust. Reps who can't see the evidence behind a field either
double-check everything by hand (defeating the point of automating it) or trust
it blindly (and inherit its mistakes silently). Confidence has to be visible per
field, not asserted once for the whole system.

**3. Extraction accuracy is usually asserted, never measured.** Teams adopt an
"AI reads your calls and updates the CRM" tool on the strength of a demo, not a
number. Nobody graded the tool against calls where the correct answer was already
known. There's no equivalent of a test suite for "does this extraction pipeline
actually work" — so the first real signal of whether it's trustworthy is a rep
finding a wrong field weeks later.

This repo's subject is those three failures: a deterministic extraction pipeline
that turns call transcripts into a structured CRM record, shows the evidence and
confidence behind every field, and is graded — automatically, on every run —
against a known-correct answer key, so "this works" is a tested claim instead of
a demo-day vibe.

### What this repo is not

- **Not connected to any other day's code.** Thematically closest to Day 018
  Duplicate CRM Graph and Day 010 CRM Doctor (CRM data quality), and a precursor
  to Day 023 Follow-Up Generator, Day 024 MEDDICC Extractor, and Day 026 Deal
  Handoff — fully standalone, no shared module, no cross-repo import. Linked from
  the README only.
- **Not a MEDDICC extractor.** Day 024 owns qualification-framework scoring
  (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, etc). This repo extracts general
  CRM fields — contacts, stage, action items, summary — not a sales
  methodology framework.
- **Not a follow-up email generator.** Day 023 owns turning meeting context into
  a follow-up draft. This repo stops at the structured record; it never
  generates outreach copy.
- **Not a deal-handoff tool.** Day 026 owns turning sales context into a
  structured handoff for implementation/CS. This repo's output is a CRM record,
  not a handoff document.
- **Not an LLM.** Extraction is a deterministic, rule-based parser over
  templated transcript text — no model call, no free-form generation, no API
  key, no per-request cost. Same spirit as the rest of Phase 1 and every day so
  far: LLM-based work is reserved for Phase 3 (Day 041+).
- **Not real call data.** A committed synthetic corpus of 50 transcripts,
  generated by a documented, seeded model, each shipped with a hidden ground-truth
  answer key the generator itself wrote in. The claim this repo makes — that
  evidence-linked, confidence-aware extraction can be measured and is honest
  about what it doesn't know — is about the *system*, not about any real
  company or call.
- **Not a CRM.** No write path, no persistence beyond the committed corpus, no
  saving edits to a record. The "try it yourself" page evaluates pasted text
  live but never stores it anywhere.

---

## Intended user

A sales/RevOps leader evaluating whether an automated call-to-CRM extraction
pipeline is trustworthy enough to rely on — who wants to see not just the output,
but the evidence behind every field, an honest confidence signal when the source
material is ambiguous, and a measured accuracy number rather than a vendor's
claim.

Secondary user: an interviewer or engineer reading the repo, who should be able
to see the corpus's generative model, every extraction rule, and the grading
logic without running anything.

---

## User journey

1. Land on the **Meeting Library**. 50 synthetic call transcripts, each already
   run through the extractor and graded against its own ground-truth answer key,
   are already computed. No upload, no sign-up, no config to set.
2. A **corpus accuracy panel** up top: overall field-level extraction accuracy
   across all 50 meetings, split by how the meetings feature a "clean" vs
   "ambiguous" flag — proving the extractor's confidence signal tracks real
   difficulty rather than being decorative.
3. **Meeting table**: every meeting's date, contacts found, inferred deal stage,
   an overall confidence badge, and its own field accuracy score. Sortable by
   accuracy or stage; filterable by stage and by clean/ambiguous.
4. Click a meeting to open its **detail page**: the full transcript on one side,
   the extracted CRM record on the other — every field shows its value,
   confidence (high/medium/low), the exact transcript line(s) it was pulled
   from, and whether it matched the meeting's ground-truth answer (correct /
   partial / missed / false positive).
5. Visit **Try It Yourself**: a textarea pre-filled with an example transcript,
   editable or replaceable with anything the visitor pastes. The same extractor
   runs client-side and shows the extracted record live — with an explicit note
   that no accuracy grade is shown here, because arbitrary pasted text has no
   known-correct answer to grade against.

## MVP scope

**In:**

- Seeded synthetic corpus: 50 call transcripts, fixed seed, each a turn-by-turn
  rep/prospect dialogue with an embedded ground-truth CRM record (the facts the
  generator deliberately wrote in), committed to the repo as JSON. Each meeting
  is independently flagged `clean` or `ambiguous` at generation time (ambiguous
  meetings deliberately omit or muddy some fact the extractor would otherwise
  find cleanly).
- A deterministic, rule-based **extractor**: parses a transcript into contacts,
  inferred deal stage, action items, a company reference, and a one-line
  summary — each field (except the summary) carrying a confidence level and
  evidence (the transcript line(s) it came from).
- A deterministic **grader**: compares an extracted record to its meeting's
  ground truth and produces a per-field match verdict plus a single 0–100 field
  accuracy score.
- Client-safe extraction: the exact same `extractRecord` function that powers
  the precomputed corpus results runs unmodified in the browser for the
  try-it-yourself page.
- Three screens: **Meeting Library** (table + corpus accuracy panel), **meeting
  detail** (transcript + extracted record + evidence + grade), and **Try It
  Yourself** (live client-side extraction, ungraded).
- `GET /api/v1/meetings`, `GET /api/schema`.
- `npm run sweep`: nine invariants, no network.

**Out (explicitly):**

- Cross-repo integration with any other day's code or data.
- Any live API call, model call, or real CRM/call data.
- MEDDICC-style qualification scoring (Day 024's territory).
- Follow-up email/draft generation (Day 023's territory).
- Deal-handoff document generation (Day 026's territory).
- Editing or saving a record — no write path, no persistence of try-it input.
- Grading of try-it-yourself input (no ground truth exists for arbitrary text).
- A separate "accuracy dashboard" screen — the library's accuracy panel and
  per-meeting scores are the whole accuracy story.

  exceptions.

  sans + JetBrains Mono for data/transcript text), same as every sibling repo.
  Confidence gets its own consistent color coding, used identically everywhere

  - **High confidence** — slate/ink (settled, unremarkable)
  - **Medium confidence** — amber (worth a second look)
  - **Low confidence / missing** — a muted red-orange (flagged, not hidden)

## Data sources

**A single committed synthetic corpus**, generated by `data/generate.ts` from a
fixed seed, checked into the repo as JSON. No real call data was considered:
this repo's claims are about whether evidence-linked, confidence-calibrated
extraction can be built and measured — properties of the extraction system, not
of which company or rep is on the transcript. The generator is committed and the
seed is fixed, so the corpus — including every ground-truth answer key — is
fully auditable.

## System / architecture

Six layers, dependency arrows pointing only downward.

```
data/                 corpus generation (transcripts + embedded ground truth, seeded RNG)
                       + committed JSON + zod load schema
  ↓
lib/domain/            Meeting, TranscriptLine, ContactFact, ActionItemFact,
                        DealStage, GroundTruthRecord, ExtractedField<T>,
                        ExtractedRecord, FieldMatch, MeetingGrade, MeetingResult,
                        CorpusAccuracy — types + zod
  ↓
lib/extraction/         extractContacts, extractDealStage, extractActionItems,
                         extractCompany, extractSummary, extractRecord(transcript)
                         — pure, rule-based, confidence-assigning functions
  ↓
lib/grading/             gradeScalarField, gradeListField, gradeRecord(extracted,
                          groundTruth) → MeetingGrade — pure functions
  ↓
lib/meeting-crm/          orchestration — runs extractRecord + gradeRecord per
                           meeting, assembles MeetingResult, aggregates
                           CorpusAccuracy
  ↓
app/                       three screens (library, meeting detail, try-it) +
                            /api/v1/meetings + /api/schema
```

### Rules of the architecture

1. **`lib/extraction/` and `lib/grading/` are pure and deterministic.** Same
   transcript ⇒ byte-identical `ExtractedRecord`; same extracted/ground-truth
   pair ⇒ byte-identical `MeetingGrade`. No `Date.now()`, no unseeded
   `Math.random()`.
2. **Nothing below `app/` knows about React, HTTP, or the DOM.** `extractRecord`
   must run identically in the browser (Try It Yourself) and on the server
   (precomputed library + API route) — it is the same imported function in both
   places, never reimplemented for either.
3. **Grading only ever runs against a meeting's own committed ground truth.**
   There is no ground truth for try-it-yourself input, so that page calls
   `extractRecord` only and never calls `gradeRecord` — this is enforced by the
   page never importing `lib/grading/`.
4. **Confidence is assigned inside `lib/extraction/`, from properties of the
   match itself** (pattern specificity, presence of a single vs. multiple
   candidates, hedging language) — never derived from whether the grader later
   judged the field correct. Confidence is a claim the extractor makes about
   itself before anyone checks its homework; grading checks that claim
   afterward. Mixing the two would make the confidence signal circular and
   worthless.

## Data model

```ts
type Speaker = 'rep' | 'prospect';
type TranscriptLine = { speaker: Speaker; speakerName: string; text: string };

type DealStage =
  | 'discovery' | 'demo' | 'proposal' | 'negotiation'
  | 'closed-won' | 'closed-lost';

type ContactFact = { name: string; role: string | null; email: string | null };
type ActionItemFact = {
  text: string;
  owner: string | null;
  dueHint: string | null;   // e.g. "by Friday" — a templated phrase, never resolved
};                            // to a real calendar date

type GroundTruthRecord = {
  contacts: ContactFact[];
  dealStage: DealStage | null;      // null = genuinely not statable from this
                                     // transcript, by design (ambiguous meetings)
  actionItems: ActionItemFact[];
  companyMentioned: string | null;
};

type FieldConfidence = 'high' | 'medium' | 'low';

type Evidence = { lineIndex: number; quote: string };

type ExtractedField<T> = {
  value: T;
  confidence: FieldConfidence;
  evidence: Evidence[];             // empty = no supporting line found
};

type ExtractedRecord = {
  contacts: ExtractedField<ContactFact>[];
  dealStage: ExtractedField<DealStage | null>;
  actionItems: ExtractedField<ActionItemFact>[];
  companyMentioned: ExtractedField<string | null>;
  summary: string;                  // templated one-liner, always generated,
                                     // never graded (no ground-truth "summary")
};

type FieldMatch = 'correct' | 'missed' | 'false-positive' | 'incorrect';

type ListFieldTally = { matched: number; missed: number; falsePositive: number };

type MeetingGrade = {
  dealStageMatch: FieldMatch;
  companyMatch: FieldMatch;
  contactsTally: ListFieldTally;
  actionItemsTally: ListFieldTally;
  fieldAccuracy: number;            // 0–100, weighted composite (see Method)
};

type Meeting = {
  id: string;
  date: string;                     // fixed synthetic date string
  ambiguityProfile: 'clean' | 'ambiguous';
  transcript: TranscriptLine[];
  groundTruth: GroundTruthRecord;
};

type MeetingResult = {
  meeting: Meeting;
  extracted: ExtractedRecord;
  grade: MeetingGrade;
};

type CorpusAccuracy = {
  meetingCount: number;
  overallFieldAccuracy: number;             // 0–100, mean of every MeetingGrade.fieldAccuracy
  byAmbiguityProfile: { clean: number; ambiguous: number }; // mean fieldAccuracy per profile
};

type MeetingCrmResult = {
  generatedAt: string;
  meetingCount: number;
  meetings: MeetingResult[];        // corpus order (by id)
  corpusAccuracy: CorpusAccuracy;
};
```

zod-enforced at every boundary: corpus load, API response.

## Method

### The corpus's generative model

`data/generate.ts` builds each of 50 meetings from independently seeded draws:
a company name, 1–3 contacts (name, role, sometimes an email), a target deal
stage, 0–3 action items, and an `ambiguityProfile` drawn `ambiguous` with
probability 0.4 (expected ~20 of 50; the sweep asserts at least 15 of each
profile so no seed can produce a degenerate split). The generator then writes a
templated, turn-by-turn transcript that *contains* those facts as dialogue —
e.g. a contact introduces themselves ("This is Priya, I'm the VP of Engineering
here"), the rep and prospect exchange stage-indicating language ("let's get a
demo on the calendar" / "we're ready to sign"), and an action item is spoken
aloud ("I'll send over the pricing sheet by Friday"). For `ambiguous` meetings,
the generator deliberately withholds or muddies one or more facts — a contact
who's never given a role, a stage discussed in contradictory terms, an action
item with no clear owner — and marks the corresponding `groundTruth` field
`null` (or omits the list entry) so the *correct* extraction is to find nothing
there, not to guess. Every draw uses a labeled, derived RNG stream (same
`Rng`/`derive` pattern as Day 019 and Day 020) so regenerating one part of the
corpus never perturbs another part's output relative to a fixed meeting id.

### Extraction rules

Each extractor function scans `TranscriptLine[]` with a small set of documented
patterns and assigns confidence from the match itself, never from grading:

```
extractContacts:
  pattern: "This is <Name>, I'm the <Role> here[ at <Company>]." — the exact
           self-introduction shape the corpus generator writes.
  email: a "you can reach me at <email>" line immediately following, attached
         by matching that line's speaker to the introduced contact's name.
  confidence: high = every extracted contact matched the full introduction
              pattern (name + role); there is no partial-confidence path — a
              contact who is never introduced by name (the `unnamed-contact`
              corpus flag) is not extracted at all, a missed field, not a
              low-confidence guess.

extractDealStage:
  keyword families, matched by scanning every line for each family's literal
  substrings:
    closed-won:    "sign the contract", "let's get started", "moving forward with you"
    closed-lost:   "going with someone else", "not moving forward", "not a fit"
    negotiation:   "legal review", "redlines", "contract terms"
    proposal:      "send over pricing", "the proposal", "quote"
    demo:          "schedule a demo", "walk you through the product"
    discovery:     (default when zero families match)
  confidence: high   = exactly one keyword family matched anywhere in the
                        transcript
              medium = zero families matched, but generic discovery/
                        exploratory language was found (defaults to discovery)
              low    = zero families matched and no discovery language either
                        (bare default), OR two or more distinct families
                        matched — a genuine conflict, value: null

extractActionItems:
  pattern: "I'll <verb phrase>[ <due clause>]." / "<Name> will <verb phrase>[ <due clause>]."
           / "Let's schedule <noun phrase>[ <due clause>]."
  owner: the speaker for "I'll…", the named person for "<Name> will…", null for
         "Let's schedule…" (no grammatical owner)
  dueHint: a trailing "by/next/within/early …" clause if present, else null
  confidence: high = both owner and dueHint resolved; medium = exactly one of
              owner/dueHint resolved; low = neither resolved. A sentence
              matching none of the three patterns (the `vague-action-item`
              corpus flag) is not extracted at all — a missed field.

extractCompany:
  pattern: the same "here at <Company>" clause captured by extractContacts'
           introduction pattern, read off the first contact that stated one.
  confidence: high if any contact's introduction stated a company; otherwise
              the field is missed (value: null, confidence: low, no bare-token
              guessing — a wrong guess would be worse than an honest miss).

extractSummary:
  a single templated sentence built from the extracted dealStage and contact
  count, e.g. "Discovery call with 2 contacts; deal stage inferred as demo." —
  descriptive only, never graded (no ground-truth "summary" field exists)
```

### Grading

```
gradeScalarField(extractedValue, groundTruthValue) → FieldMatch:
  both null                     → 'correct'   (correctly abstained)
  groundTruth set, extracted =  → 'correct'
  groundTruth set, extracted null → 'missed'
  groundTruth null, extracted set → 'false-positive'
  groundTruth set, extracted set, unequal → 'incorrect'

gradeListField(extracted[], groundTruth[]) → ListFieldTally:
  match by normalized-name (contacts) or normalized-text (action items) equality
  matched        = count present in both
  missed         = count in groundTruth, absent from extracted
  falsePositive  = count in extracted, absent from groundTruth

fieldAccuracy(grade) (0–100):
  scalarScore(match) = match === 'correct' ? 100 : 0
  listScore(tally)   = (tally.matched + tally.missed + tally.falsePositive) === 0
                         ? 100
                         : round(100 * tally.matched /
                             (tally.matched + tally.missed + tally.falsePositive))

  fieldAccuracy = round(
    0.30 * scalarScore(dealStageMatch) +
    0.15 * scalarScore(companyMatch)   +
    0.30 * listScore(contactsTally)    +
    0.25 * listScore(actionItemsTally)
  )
```

Weights (30/15/30/25) reflect that deal stage and contacts are the fields a CRM
record is least usable without, company reference matters but is usually
redundant with account context a real CRM already has, and action items are
important but partial credit already comes from the list-based scoring.

## Meeting to CRM (app)

Three screens, all reading from one precomputed `MeetingCrmResult` for the
library and detail pages — no upload, no key, no empty state needed for the
happy path.

1. **Meeting Library** (`/`) — corpus accuracy panel (overall field accuracy,

   accuracy or stage; filterable by stage and by clean/ambiguous profile.
2. **Meeting detail** (`/meetings/[id]`) — transcript on one side (each line
   attributed to rep/prospect), extracted record on the other: every field
   shows its value, a confidence badge, its evidence line(s) (clicking jumps to

   false positive / incorrect) against ground truth.

   transcript, freely editable/replaceable. Runs `extractRecord` client-side on
   submit and renders the same extracted-record view as the detail page, minus
   any grade badges, with a visible note explaining why no accuracy grade is
   shown for arbitrary input.

## Validation / test plan

**Unit tests** (`vitest`, `lib/**/*.test.ts`, `data/**/*.test.ts`): each
extraction rule against hand-built transcript lines with known expected
fields/confidence; grading logic against hand-built extracted/ground-truth
pairs covering every `FieldMatch` case; `fieldAccuracy`'s weighted formula
against known expected scores; evidence traceability (every evidence entry's
`lineIndex` is valid and its `quote` is a substring of that line); corpus
structural assertions (meeting count, ambiguity split, id uniqueness).

**`npm run sweep`** — no network, asserting **nine invariants**:

1. **Corpus size.** Exactly 50 meetings, unique ids, each with at least one
   transcript line.
2. **Ambiguity mix.** At least 15 meetings are `clean` and at least 15 are
   `ambiguous`.
3. **Field bounds.** Every `ExtractedField.confidence` is one of
   `high`/`medium`/`low`; every `MeetingGrade.fieldAccuracy` and
   `CorpusAccuracy.overallFieldAccuracy` is an integer in `[0, 100]`.
4. **Evidence traceability.** Every non-empty evidence entry's `lineIndex` is a
   valid index into that meeting's transcript, and its `quote` is a substring
   of that line's `text`.
5. **Grading reproducibility.** Recomputing `gradeRecord(extracted,
   groundTruth)` from scratch for every meeting matches the precomputed
   `MeetingResult.grade` exactly — no drift between orchestration and the pure
   grading function.
6. **Confidence calibration.** Across the corpus, the mean scalar-field
   correctness rate (`dealStageMatch`/`companyMatch` both `'correct'`) for
   `high`-confidence fields is strictly greater than for `low`-confidence
   fields — proving confidence predicts real correctness rather than being
   decorative.
7. **Difficulty realism.** `CorpusAccuracy.byAmbiguityProfile.ambiguous` is
   strictly less than `byAmbiguityProfile.clean` — proving the `ambiguous` flag
   corresponds to genuinely harder extraction, not a label with no effect.
8. **Competence floor.** `CorpusAccuracy.overallFieldAccuracy >= 75` — the
   extractor is basically competent across the whole corpus, even with
   deliberately engineered ambiguity dragging the average down.
9. **Determinism.** Running corpus generation and the full extraction+grading
   pipeline twice over the same seed produces byte-identical output
   (`JSON.stringify` equality).

**Manual verification** before shipping: the main journey end to end on the
deployed URL — library loads with the accuracy panel populated and the table
sorted/filterable, a meeting detail page renders transcript + extracted record
+ evidence + grade badges for at least one `clean` and one `ambiguous` meeting,
the Try It Yourself page extracts live from the prefilled example and from a
freely typed replacement transcript with no console errors. Failure states: a
meeting with an intentionally-unresolved field (`dealStage: null`,
`companyMentioned: null`, or an empty `actionItems`) renders a clear "not
found" state for that field rather than a blank or crash; a try-it transcript
with no extractable fields at all still renders a valid (all-low-confidence,
empty-evidence) record without crashing; an unknown `/meetings/[id]` renders a
clear not-found state, not a crash.

## Deployment plan

Vercel, linked at task 1 so the live URL exists before the README needs it. No
environment variables, no secrets, no external calls at runtime — the corpus is
committed, so the deployment is static plus pure client/server compute. `main`

"live deployment" screenshot is taken or the build is called done — this bug
was caught and fixed on Day 020 (`gtm-command-center`) and is still unfixed on

## Settled decisions

Every one of these was put to the user and confirmed before code was written,
through a structured grilling session.

1. **Standalone repo**, fresh synthetic corpus, zero cross-repo dependency —
   thematically linked to `crm-duplicate-graph`, `crm-doctor`, and (later)
   `follow-up-generator`, `meddicc-extractor`, `deal-handoff` in the README
   only; deliberately scoped to stay general-purpose so it doesn't cannibalize
   those later days' portfolio angles.
2. **Extraction is synthetic + deterministic, not a live LLM call.** Matches
   the zero-live-dependency convention held by all 20 prior days; extraction
   logic is rule-based pattern matching over templated transcript text, not
   general natural-language understanding.
3. **Input material is a turn-by-turn call transcript** (rep/prospect dialogue
   with speaker attribution), not raw rep notes.
4. **Extracted record is general-purpose CRM fields**: contacts, inferred deal
   stage, action items, company reference, one-line summary — plus evidence
   and confidence per field. Explicitly not MEDDICC scoring, follow-up
   drafting, or handoff structuring.
5. **Corpus scale is 50 synthetic meetings** — enough to demonstrate table/
   filter UX and a meaningful accuracy split, small enough that full
   transcripts stay a reasonable scope for a one-day build.
6. **Corpus deliberately mixes clean and ambiguous meetings** (~40%
   ambiguous), so confidence flags and grading failures are a real,
   demonstrated feature rather than dead code.
7. **The generator embeds a hidden ground-truth answer key per meeting**, and
   the app grades every extraction against it, surfacing a measured field
   accuracy score per meeting and corpus-wide — turning the "transforms into
   structured info" claim into a falsifiable, tested one.
8. **Two interaction modes**: a browsable precomputed corpus (library +
   detail, matching the series' Command-Center-style pattern) plus a "try it
   yourself" page running the identical extractor client-side on
   user-pasted text — with no accuracy grade shown there, since arbitrary
   pasted text has no ground truth to grade against.
9. Stack, deployment, license, and repo visibility are **identical to the
   sibling repos**: Next.js App Router, TypeScript strict, Tailwind CSS 4,
   zod, vitest/vite-node, zero dependency exceptions, deployed to Vercel,
   pushed to `github.com/akshatiwarix`, MIT license, public repo.
10. **Time limit is one day**, matching the rest of the series.
11. Push to `main` after **every** completed task.
